We need to make a substantial storefront design and UX update for Bee Hairplace.

The objective is to replicate the visual design, page structure, and shopping experience of the reference website screenshots I provided, adapted for Bee Hairplace.

This is NOT a request to invent a new design direction.

The reference screenshots are our primary visual source of truth. Study them carefully and reproduce their layout, section order, proportions, typography, colors, spacing, product presentation, and mobile behavior as closely as reasonably possible.

At the same time, this is an existing application. We must preserve its working architecture, backend, product data, Supabase integration, media processing, admin functionality, and Paystack checkout.

Do not start coding blindly. Inspect the current implementation, understand its structure, then implement the redesign in deliberate passes.

2. DESIGN OBJECTIVE: REPLICATE THE REFERENCE
==================================================

The reference is a conventional fashion and hair e-commerce storefront.

Its visual direction includes:

- A predominantly white or very light background.
- Pink/magenta as the primary accent for important buttons and interactive elements.
- Dark text with elegant serif-style headings.
- A compact header with a logo, cart indicator, and hamburger menu on mobile.
- Large hair imagery and promotional banners.
- Clearly separated homepage sections.
- Conventional product cards with product media, name, price, and Add to cart.
- A traditional product-detail page with a gallery, product information, options, and purchase controls.
- A multi-section footer with contact information and useful navigation links.

The result should feel like a polished hair e-commerce website, not a generic SaaS landing page or a minimal editorial portfolio.

Do not introduce a completely different visual language.

Avoid:
- Replacing the reference layout with a new layout you personally prefer.
- Excessive gradients, glassmorphism, floating cards, or decorative effects absent from the reference.
- Unnecessary animation.
- Excessive rounded containers.
- Large empty areas that make the page feel unfinished.
- Repeated calls to action that add no value.
- Generic luxury copy that does not communicate anything useful.
- Making Bee herself the entire story of the store.

Bee Hairplace should feel like a real hair brand and online store. Bee can appear naturally in the product videos and imagery, but do not repeatedly use phrases such as “Bee is the catalogue,” “Worn by Bee,” or “Modelled by Bee” as the central brand message.

Use the reference's visual hierarchy while keeping the copy appropriate to Bee Hairplace.
