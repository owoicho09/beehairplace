I want to build a production-quality, mobile-first e-commerce website for a Nigerian hair vendor called Bee Hairplace.

Do not start coding yet.

Your first task is to deeply reason through the product, architecture, UX and implementation plan based on the context below.

I have already spent time thinking through the product and making important product decisions, but I do NOT want you to blindly translate this into code or treat every detail as a rigid technical instruction.

Understand what we are trying to achieve, challenge weak implementation assumptions where necessary, identify technical risks, and propose the cleanest practical architecture for the MVP.

The eventual goal is to build the complete application after we agree on the plan.

PRODUCT

Bee Hairplace is a personal-brand hair business in Abuja, Nigeria.

The owner, Bee, models/wears the wigs herself in short portrait videos. This is already how her audience experiences and discovers her products on social media.

The website should turn that existing behavior into a proper e-commerce experience.

The defining idea is:

This is a video-first hair store where Bee herself is the visual catalogue.

It should NOT feel like a generic Shopify-style hair store filled with static product cards.

At the same time, it must be fast, especially on mobile. We specifically do not want a page trying to autoplay 30–50+ product videos.

Most customers will visit from mobile devices.

CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

There is no customer registration or customer account system.

A customer should simply:

browse → find a hair → open it → select their option/length → buy → enter checkout details → pay → receive confirmation.

Do not introduce authentication or account creation into the customer journey.

HOMEPAGE

The homepage should feel like Bee's digital showroom rather than a generic e-commerce template.

The hero should use a strong video of Bee as the background.

Hero video behavior:
- autoplay
- muted
- looping
- inline
- no visible video controls

The page should visually prioritize Bee, the hair and movement over excessive copy.

Likely homepage content includes:

- minimal navigation
- video hero
- shop/category discovery
- a very small featured/new-hair section
- personal-brand/editorial section around Bee
- best sellers or another curated product section
- physical Abuja store section
- social proof/customer content if real content is available
- basic delivery/payment reassurance
- final shopping CTA
- footer

Do not blindly create sections just because e-commerce templates normally have them.

Avoid generic "Why Choose Us" icon sections, fake statistics, fake testimonials, huge About Us sections, excessive FAQ content, generic marketing copy and unnecessary UI.

FEATURED VIDEOS

The homepage may have approximately 3 featured product videos.

These can autoplay muted and loop because there are very few of them and they are intentionally being used to establish the visual identity of the store.

Do not extend this behavior to the full catalogue.

CATALOGUE / SHOP

The shop should be optimized for extremely fast visual browsing.

On mobile, use a dense 3-column portrait grid inspired by the browsing density of a TikTok profile grid, but do not clone TikTok's UI.

Each product card should show only:

- product visual/poster
- product name
- price / starting price

Nothing else.

All additional information belongs on the product detail page.

The catalogue should NOT autoplay every product video.

There could eventually be 30–50 products and later significantly more.

Instead, products should have lightweight poster images automatically generated from their uploaded videos.

The vendor should NOT have to upload thumbnails manually.

The catalogue therefore behaves primarily like an optimized image grid.

A subtle indicator can communicate that the product contains video.

Tapping the product opens its detail page.

Search and filtering must actually work.

Categories are dynamic and admin-managed rather than hardcoded.

Filtering should be designed appropriately for mobile rather than squeezing a desktop filter sidebar onto the screen.

VIDEO / MEDIA

Video is the primary product media.

A product video is required.

Additional product photos are optional.

The admin should only need to upload the source video.

The system should take responsibility for creating whatever optimized media it needs, including an automatically generated catalogue poster/thumbnail.

Think carefully about how video processing should work.

We care about:

- mobile performance
- bandwidth
- storage
- maintaining enough video quality to properly see hair texture and movement
- automatic poster generation
- not loading video unnecessarily
- avoiding huge raw phone-video files being served directly
- graceful processing failures
- cleaning up replaced/deleted media
- keeping the media architecture replaceable if storage requirements grow later

Do not assume a particular processing implementation simply because I mentioned these requirements.

Reason about the simplest reliable MVP implementation.

We are likely to start with Supabase for Postgres and possibly Supabase Storage.

However, do not tightly couple the entire application to one storage provider if a small abstraction makes future migration straightforward.

We expect the store to start relatively small but it could eventually reach 100–200+ products.

PRODUCT DETAIL

Opening a product should move from fast catalogue browsing into a richer product experience.

The product video should dominate the product detail page.

Unlike catalogue/homepage autoplay videos, this is a proper video player.

It should support:
- sound
- play/pause
- seeking/rewinding
- mute/unmute
- fullscreen where supported

Do not force autoplay with sound.

Product information can include:

- name
- price
- available lengths/options
- description
- availability
- optional supporting photos

The important purchase controls should appear before long descriptive content.

A product can have different prices for different lengths.

The admin must not be restricted to predefined length checkboxes.

She should be able to enter the lengths/options herself.

Some products may not require length variants at all, so design the data model sensibly rather than forcing every product into the exact same variant structure.

The page should support Buy Now and Add to Bag.

RELATED PRODUCTS

At the bottom of the product page, recommend other relevant hairs.

On mobile these should be a horizontal swipeable row/carousel rather than another long vertical product grid.

Related cards should retain the same visual language:
poster + name + price.

They do not need to autoplay.

CART

Keep the cart simple.

Use generated product posters rather than loading videos.

Customers should be able to:
- review products
- see selected length/variant
- change quantity
- remove items
- see subtotal
- proceed to checkout

CHECKOUT

No authentication.

Collect only the information required to fulfil the order, such as:

- full name
- email
- phone/WhatsApp number
- fulfilment method
- delivery information when applicable

There are two fulfilment methods:

1. Delivery
2. Pickup from the physical Abuja store

If pickup is selected, unnecessary delivery address fields should disappear.

Delivery pricing/rules should be configurable from admin rather than hardcoded into the storefront.

PAYMENTS

Payments will use Paystack.

Think through this securely.

Do not trust frontend payment state.

Orders/payment initialization and verification should be handled appropriately server-side, including Paystack webhook verification.

The application should distinguish payment status from fulfilment/order status.

Do not implement transaction splitting or platform commissions at this stage.

After successful payment, show a proper order confirmation page with an order reference and summary.

WhatsApp can be used as an additional support/communication channel after purchase.

ADMIN

There is only one store administrator for the MVP.

We do NOT need:
- admin registration
- multiple organizations
- staff invitations
- roles/permissions
- complex account management

However, admin credentials must not be exposed in frontend source code.

Design the simplest secure server-side admin session/login appropriate for this architecture.

The admin itself must work well on mobile because Bee may manage products directly from her phone.

The main admin areas should conceptually cover:

- overview
- products
- categories
- orders
- homepage merchandising/content
- delivery configuration
- basic store settings

Do not create an unnecessarily complex analytics dashboard.

PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

Adding a product should be very simple.

The admin should be able to provide:

- name
- category
- required video
- optional photos
- optional description
- base price or length/variant pricing
- availability
- publishing/display settings

The system should handle media optimization/poster generation behind the scenes.

The UI should communicate media processing states clearly.

Categories should be manageable by the admin.

The admin should be able to create a new category while adding/editing a product, and that category should then become available throughout the store and filters.

Useful product actions can include things such as edit, duplicate, publish/unpublish, availability and delete/archive.

Think carefully about deletion because historical orders must remain meaningful even if a product is later renamed or removed.

Orders should preserve/snapshot the important purchased information rather than depending entirely on the current product record.

HOMEPAGE MANAGEMENT

Bee should be able to manage the business content that naturally changes, without being able to accidentally redesign the website.

For example, she may need control over:

- hero video
- featured products
- best sellers
- store video
- relevant store/contact information

The featured autoplay product section should remain intentionally small (around three products).

The admin controls content and commerce, not fonts, colors, layout systems or arbitrary design configuration.

ORDERS

The admin should be able to search and manage orders.

Keep payment status separate from order/fulfilment status.

Useful fulfilment states may include concepts such as:
- processing
- ready for pickup
- out for delivery
- completed
- cancelled

Do not blindly use these exact values if you identify a cleaner state model.

VISUAL DIRECTION

The storefront should feel like:

premium fashion editorial × modern social commerce.

It should NOT look like:
- generic Shopify theme
- generic AI-generated website
- SaaS dashboard
- black-and-gold "luxury" cliché
- gradient-heavy landing page

The UI should deliberately get out of the way of Bee and the hair.

Explore a restrained warm-neutral palette:
- warm ivory/off-white backgrounds
- near-black text/actions
- subtle warm greys
- possibly a restrained burgundy/brown brand accent

The hair and videos should provide most of the color.

Typography can combine:
- an elegant editorial serif for selective brand statements/headlines
- a highly readable modern sans-serif for commerce/UI

Do not overuse the serif.

Product catalogue:
- dense
- clean
- minimal gaps
- portrait imagery
- no unnecessary borders
- no giant rounded cards
- no heavy shadows
- name + price only

Avoid excessive animations.

The videos themselves already provide movement.

Use animation only where it improves interaction: drawers, sheets, transitions, cart feedback, etc.

PHYSICAL STORE

Bee Hairplace has a physical store in Abuja.

The homepage should include a credibility section featuring a video of the actual store.

The store video can autoplay muted/looped without controls when the section approaches the viewport.

Store address/contact/opening-hour information should come from store settings rather than being duplicated throughout source code.

WHATSAPP

WhatsApp is important for customers who need reassurance/questions before purchasing.

Integrate it tastefully.

It should complement checkout rather than replacing the e-commerce experience.

Do not allow a giant floating button to constantly obstruct products on small screens.

TECHNICAL DIRECTION

I plan to use a modern Next.js application.

Supabase is likely for Postgres and may initially handle media storage.

Paystack handles payment.

I want the application to be deployable using practical low-cost infrastructure appropriate for an MVP.

Environment-specific credentials must be stored through environment variables.

Do not expose service credentials/secrets to the browser.

We want clean separation between:
- storefront
- admin
- database/business logic
- media handling
- payments

But do not over-engineer this into microservices unless there is an actual reason.

The site must be responsive, accessible and particularly good on mobile.

PERFORMANCE IS A PRODUCT REQUIREMENT

Do not treat performance as something to fix after building.

Think about:

- what loads above the fold
- lazy loading
- generated posters
- video preload behavior
- media compression
- caching
- responsive images
- database query patterns
- catalogue pagination/loading strategy
- search/filter behavior
- bandwidth
- Core Web Vitals
- slower Nigerian/mobile network conditions

The hero video should receive priority over video much further down the homepage.

A store video near the bottom should not compete with the hero during initial page load.

Likewise, the catalogue should not download dozens of videos merely because their posters are visible.
